# 01.00 Business Model Options

The "best" way to model a business depends on whether you are trying to **organize your daily tasks**, **align a large team**, or **prepare for massive scaling**.

If you are looking for alternatives to the "Standard 6" (where the 6th is **Finance**), here are the most effective frameworks categorized by their specific purpose.

---

### 1. The "Lean" Model (4 Pillars)

**Best for:** Solopreneurs, freelancers, or early-stage startups who need to move fast without over-complicating things.

* **Product:** What you sell and the value it provides.
* **Marketing/Sales:** How people find you and how you close the deal.
* **Operations:** How you fulfill the work (the "doing").
* **Finance:** How you track the money and ensure you stay profitable.

### 2. The "EOS" Model (6 Components)

**Best for:** Growing agencies or businesses that feel chaotic and need "Traction." This is based on the *Entrepreneurial Operating System*.

| Component | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Vision** | Getting everyone 100% on the same page with where you're going. |
| **People** | Ensuring you have the "Right People in the Right Seats." |
| **Data** | Cutting through personalities and egos to run the business on a few key numbers. |
| **Issues** | Identifying and solving problems permanently (rather than just talking about them). |
| **Process** | Systematizing the "core" of the business to create consistency. |
| **Traction** | Bringing the vision down to the ground through discipline and accountability. |

### 3. The McKinsey 7S Model (7 Pillars)

**Best for:** Mid-to-large organizations where the "culture" or "vibe" is getting in the way of the strategy. It focuses on **Alignment**.

* **Hard Elements:** Strategy, Structure, Systems.
* **Soft Elements:** Shared Values (the center), Style (leadership), Staff, Skills.

> **Key Insight:** In this model, "Shared Values" sits in the center. If the other 6 pillars aren't aligned with the central values, the business will eventually "vibrate apart" under stress.

---

### 4. The "Agency" Scaling Model (5 Pillars)

Since you mentioned "Growth" and "Performance," you might prefer this version, which is specifically designed for service-based businesses:

1. **Lead Generation:** Filling the pipeline.
2. **Lead Conversion:** Turning leads into clients.
3. **Delivery/Fulfillment:** Doing the work (Operations).
4. **Retention/LTV:** Keeping clients and getting referrals.
5. **Finance/Admin:** Managing the "engine" (billing, taxes, legal).

---

### Which one should you choose?

* If you feel like you're **working too much *in* the business** and not *on* it: Use the **EOS Model**.
* If you are **struggling with cash flow** despite having sales: Use the **Agency Scaling Model** with a heavy focus on Finance.
* If you are a **one-person show** just starting out: Stick to the **Lean 4 Pillars**.
